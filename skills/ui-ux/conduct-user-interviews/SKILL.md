# Conduct User Interviews

User interviews are a fundamental user research method for gathering deep insights into user needs, motivations, behaviors, and pain points. This skill provides a systematic approach to planning, conducting, and analyzing effective user interviews.

## Why
Conducting user interviews poorly leads to:
- **Biased data**: Leading questions or poor interviewing techniques skew results
- **Missed opportunities**: Failing to probe deeply enough misses critical insights
- **Wasted time**: Poorly structured interviews yield unusable or unfocused data
- **Ethical issues**: Not respecting participants' time or privacy
- **False confidence**: Superficial interviews create illusion of understanding without real insights

## Do
- **Define clear research goals**:
  - What specific questions do you need answered?
  - How will the insights be used in the design process?
  - What decisions will this research inform?

- **Create an interview guide**:
  - Start with warm-up questions to build rapport
  - Use open-ended questions that encourage storytelling
  - Follow a logical flow from general to specific
  - Include probes to dig deeper ("Can you tell me more about that?")
  - End with wrap-up questions and an opportunity for additional thoughts
  - Keep guide flexible—follow interesting threads where they lead

- **Recruit representative participants**:
  - Define user segments based on behaviors, needs, or contexts
  - Use screening questionnaires to ensure relevant participants
  - Offer appropriate incentives for participation time
  - Consider accessibility needs in recruitment and compensation

- **Follow interviewing best practices**:
  - Build rapport at the beginning—be friendly, empathetic, and professional
  - Get informed consent and explain how data will be used
  - Actively listen—focus on understanding, not just waiting to talk
  - Use neutral language—avoid leading questions or expressing judgment
  - Follow the participant's lead—explore what they find important
  - Use silence effectively—give participants time to think and elaborate
  - Avoid filling every pause—sometimes the best insights come after hesitation
  - Record with permission (audio/video) and take notes
  - Have a notetaker if possible so you can focus on listening

- **Probe effectively**:
  - Use the "5 Whys" technique to get to root causes
  - Ask for specific examples rather than generalizations
  - Explore emotions and motivations, not just behaviors
  - Ask about workarounds or coping strategies users have developed
  - Inquire about ideal states or wish lists
  - Validate understanding by summarizing what you heard

- **Analyze and synthesize findings**:
  - Review recordings and notes soon after interviews while details are fresh
  - Look for patterns across participants, not just individual quotes
  - Use affinity diagrams to organize qualitative data
  - Identify recurring themes, pain points, motivations, and behaviors
  - Create personas, journey maps, or opportunity frameworks from research
  - Share findings in accessible formats that drive action

## Don't
- Don't ask leading questions that prompt desired responses
- Don't dominate the conversation—aim for 80% participant talking, 20% interviewer
- Don't interrupt or finish participants' sentences
- Don't stick rigidly to the guide if interesting topics emerge
- Don't judge or express shock at participants' responses
- Don't interview colleagues, friends, or family unless they truly represent target users
- Don't record without explicit permission
- Don't share identifiable information without consent
- Don't rush through interviews—quality insights take time
- Don't fail to debrief and capture impressions immediately after each interview

## Failure Modes
| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Insights feel superficial | Didn't probe deeply enough or ask follow-up questions | Use probing techniques and follow interesting threads |
| Data is contradictory or confusing | Didn't clarify ambiguities or resolve contradictions during interview | Ask for clarification and examples in real-time |
| Participants go off-topic | Interview lacked clear structure or focus | Use gentle redirection to maintain focus on research goals |
| Interviewer bias influenced responses | Nonverbal cues or tone conveyed expectations | Practice neutral facilitation and self-awareness |
| Missed nonverbal cues | Too focused on notes or guide rather than participant | Practice active listening and consider using a notetaker |
| Ethical concerns arose | Didn't obtain proper consent or protect privacy | Follow institutional review board guidelines or company policies |
| Analysis was overwhelming | Didn't organize or prioritize findings effectively | Use affinity diagramming and thematic analysis approaches |
| Insights weren't actionable | Findings weren't connected to design decisions | Structure research around specific design questions |

## Verifying
- Check interview guide: confirm it has clear goals, open-ended questions, and logical flow
- Review consent process: verify informed consent was obtained and recorded
- Observe interviewing technique: ensure active listening, neutral facilitation, and effective probing
- Examine notes and recordings: look for evidence of deep exploration and specific examples
- Validate analysis process: confirm patterns are based on multiple data points, not isolated quotes
- Assess actionability: verify insights connect to specific design decisions or opportunities
- Review ethical compliance: check consent procedures, data protection, and compensation practices
